Grammar the engine understands

  • to A for life / to A for the life of B / to A for 10 years → duration of the estate
  • to A / to A and his heirs / to A in fee simple → fee simple
  • Durational words (so long as, while, until, during) → determinable / automatic cut-off
  • Condition-subsequent words (but if, provided that, on condition that, unless, however, if) → cut short by exercise of a power or by a gift over
  • Condition precedent: a bare if / when / upon / after attached to a gift with no comma (then to B if B reaches 21)
  • Gift over: , to C / , then to C / , in which case to C, or a reversionary clause such as O may re-enter
  • Chained conditions: and, or, and if, or if → parsed into an AND/OR tree
  • Negative conditions: not, never, fails to, ceases to, no longer, unless
  • Time conditions: age 21, reaches/turns/attains, survives, upon A's death, five years after (a gap creates a springing interest)

Punctuation matters, exactly as it does on an exam: the engine uses the comma rule to separate a condition precedent from a condition subsequent.

Study aid only. Applies common-law classification; does not apply modern statutory abolitions, the Rule in Shelley's Case, or the Doctrine of Worthier Title.
